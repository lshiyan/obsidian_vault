- MAIN IDEA: Economic based credit assignment strategies allow agents to learn to self-orchestrate.
- Organization in multi-agent systems is often an inherent property of the system, so it is interesting to ask whether or not agent swarms can learn to self-organize to increase their efficacies.
- The setting is episodic tasks $x$ with verifiable rewards, which unfold over a series of $T$ time steps. At each timestep, each agent independently submits a bid $b_i$ in order to act at a given turn, and the selected bid is denoted $b_t$. 
- At time $t$, defined the total environment reward obtained as $\hat{r}_t$, and the accumulated value as $v_t$, where $v_t = \hat{r}_t + b_{t+1}v_{t+1}$. The local training credit $\tilde{r}_t$ is given as $\tilde{r}_t = (1-b_t)v_t$ for the agent that is acting at time $t$. Essentially an agent who bids higher gets the privilege of acting, but is also willing to give up more of the credit to the prior contributors. Analogous to an auction, bidding higher is more "expensive" for the acting agent. Each agent is then optimized using a standard REINFORCE policy gradient algorithm $\nabla_{\theta_i} J = \mathbb{E}_{t \in W_i} \left[ (\tilde{r}_t - \bar{r}_i) \nabla_{\theta_i} \log \pi_i(a_t \mid h_{i,t}) \right]$, where $W_i$ represents the turns in which agent $i$ is acting, $h_{i,t}$ is the historical trajectory of agent $i$ at turn $t$, and the advantage is calculated relative to the average local reward over such turn.
- The agents are trained to self-orchestrate through a counterfactual rollout. A given decision point is defined as "contested" if the runner-up selects a non-zero bid. We restore the state to a given decision point, and then rollout with the counterfactual bid to obtain a preferred bid $y_w$ and a rejected bid $y_l$. The agent is then trained to bid with the Bradley-Terry preference objective $\mathcal{L}_{\text{bid}} = -\log \sigma\left(\log \pi_i(y_w \mid h_{i,t}) - \log \pi_i(y_l \mid h_{i,t})\right)$. They train 4 action optimization steps and then 1 bidding optimization step.

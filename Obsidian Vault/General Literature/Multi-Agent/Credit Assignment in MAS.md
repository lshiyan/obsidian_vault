- LLM agents are reading and writing to a shared context, at a decision point $u$, an agent reads the historical context $h_u$ and writes an action $a_u$, where $h_u = \psi(\tau_{\lt u})$ for a deterministic assembly map $\psi$. At $u$, an agent $i$ performs the action as $a_u = \pi_{\theta}^i(\cdot | h_u)$, and at the end of the trajectory a scalar reward is given.
- The advantage of a decision point is given by $A(u) = \mathbb{E}_{D_b}\left[R(\tau) \mid h_u, a_u\right] - \mathbb{E}_{D_b}\left[R(\tau) \mid h_u\right]$, where $D_b$ is the distribution induced by the behavior policy $\pi_b$.
- C3 first performs counterfactual sampling of messages. For each iteration, a fixed behavior policy $\pi_b$ is taken from $\pi_{\theta}$, and at each decision point, a group of counterfactual messages is sampled from the policy. Each of these alternative messages is given $c_j$ independent rollouts. This gives us an unbiased Q-value estimate $\hat{Q}_{D_b}(h_u,a_j)=\frac{1}{c_j}\sum_{m=1}^{c_j}R_m$. For each rollout action $a_j$, we baseline it with the mean reward value of the other rollouts, and then compute the advantage by subtracting the Q-value estimate from the baseline $b_{-j} = \frac{1}{C-c_j}\sum_{k\neq j}c_k\hat{Q}_{D_b}(h_u,a_k), \qquad \hat{A}_j = \hat{Q}_{D_b}(h_u,a_j)-b_{-j}$. PPO is then used to optimize the policy.

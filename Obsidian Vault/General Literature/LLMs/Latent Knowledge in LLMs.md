- MAIN IDEA: LLMs might natively encode an aspect of truth in their internal representations that are not necessarily present in their outputs.
- We are given a set of natural language questions $q_i, ... q_n$, and we want to answer them correctly. We do not want to use any actual output of the LLM to solve it, instead simply relying on the latent feature space $\phi(x)$.
- For each question, we answer 'yes' and 'no' to each, computing the internals representations $\phi(x_i^+)$ and $\phi(x_i^-)$, and then mapping these representations to probabilities $p_i^{+/-}$.
- After we have the internal representations $\phi(x_i^+)$ and $\phi(x_i^-)$, we first normalize them to bring them into the same cluster $\tilde{\phi(x_i^+)} = \phi(x_i^+) - \mu^+$, $\tilde{\phi(x_i^-)} = \phi(x_i^-) - \mu^-$. Then, we use a learned probe $p_{\theta, b}(\tilde{\phi})$ to output the probability that a given representation is true. 
- We define two loss functions, the first one is consistency loss, which just represents that probabilities must add to unity $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{consistency}}(\theta,b;q_i) := \left[ p_{\theta,b}\!\left(x_i^{+}\right) - \left(1-p_{\theta,b}\!\left(x_i^{-}\right)\right) \right]^2$. The next one is the confidence loss, which specifies that the statement that is false should have as little probability as possible $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{confidence}}(\theta,b;q_i) := \left[ \min\left\{ p_{\theta,b}\!\left(x_i^{+}\right), p_{\theta,b}\!\left(x_i^{-}\right) \right\} \right]^2$. The final loss is a weighted sum of the two $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{CCS}}(\theta,b) := \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^{n} \left[ \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{consistency}}(\theta,b;q_i) + \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{confidence}}(\theta,b;q_i) \right]$.
- At inference time, we take $0.5(p_i^+ (1-p_i^-))$ as the probability that a statement is true.
